# Corvid Cartography — pylibstats

**The family.** OldCrow's libraries share one visual discipline, established
by the corvus piece: a single corvid held against a field of patient
measurement. Ink-dark ground (`#161b23`), marks in the cream of old survey
paper (`#e7e0cf`), a tarnished gold (`#c8a24e`) spent only on what has been
fixed and named. One display serif for one word; a mono whisper for
everything numbered.

**The supplement.** A binding is not a new species, so this is not a new
plate — it is plate 2 reissued, the way a field guide issues supplementary
plates. The magpie, the specimen tray, the seven gold pins, and the
constellation *Pica* are unchanged from the libstats plate. Three things
are new.

**The python.** A second collector rests along the tray frame opposite the
magpie, head raised, regarding the same hoard. It is drawn in the same
paper-cut silhouette and granted the same courtesies: calm, still, part of
the diagram. The two never interact — they share a collection, which is
what a binding is.

**The annotation.** The plate's mono whisper now speaks Python: one REPL
line, `>>> Gaussian(μ, σ).fit(x)`, where the parent plate had none. Same
measurements, new interface.

**The caption.** `fig. 2a — the same specimens, importable`. The "a"
suffix marks the supplement; the guide now reads 1, 2, 2a, 3, 3a.
